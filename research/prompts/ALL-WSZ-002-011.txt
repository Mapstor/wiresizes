   - Inputs:
     · ocpdA: a rating from nec-240-6-a.json between 15 and 400;
     · loadA: optional; defaults to ocpdA; must not exceed ocpdA;
     · lengthFt: one way, 0 to 2000;
     · volts, under the input rules above;
     · phase: single or three;
     · material: Cu or Al;
     · vdTargetPct: 1 to 10, default 3.
   - Invalid input returns { error } with one of these codes, never a size, checked in this order (the first failure wins): 'rating-not-standard', 'rating-out-of-range', 'load-above-rating', 'length-out-of-range', 'vd-target-out-of-range', 'voltage-invalid'.
   - Step 1, ampacity: take the smallest nec-310-16.json size whose 60 °C ampacity for the material (cu_60 or al_60) is at least ocpdA. Skip sizes with no 60 °C value.
   - Step 2, 240.4(D): call maxOcpd240_4D(size, material) (see 6c). If it returns a row with conditions null and ocpdA exceeds its max_ocpd_a, move up to the next size with a 60 °C value until it doesn't. In the 60 °C column this never changes the size; it guards the later 75 °C option.
   - Step 3, EGC: take the first nec-250-122.json row whose ocpd_a_not_exceeding is at least ocpdA. Use the cu column for copper circuits and al_cca for aluminum.
   - Step 4, voltage drop at loadA, with 75 °C resistance from awg.json:
     · copper AWG: r75_cu_solid_ohm_per_1000ft_derived;
     · copper kcmil: r75_cu_solid_equiv_ohm_per_1000ft_derived;
     · aluminum: r75_al_ech19_ohm_per_1000ft_derived.
     VD = k × I × R × L / 1000, where k = 2 for single-phase and √3 for three-phase. % = VD / volts × 100.
   - Step 5: if the % is above vdTargetPct, step to the next larger nec-310-16 size until it meets the target or reaches 2000 kcmil. In that case return the 2000 kcmil result with meetsTarget false. Never fall back to a smaller size.
   - Step 6: if step 5 upsized, the required EGC area is tableEgcArea × chosenArea ÷ step2Area, multiplied first. awg.json areas are whole numbers, so the product is exact and the division is correctly rounded. (Computing the ratio first gives 211600.00000000003 cmil for a 4/0 circuit and picks a 250 kcmil ground wire.) Pick the smallest nec-310-16 size with at least that area, never smaller than the table EGC. This is the 250.122(B) rule in nec-250-122.json (rule_250_122_b). Table 250.122 can give an EGC larger than the conductor: a 25 A aluminum circuit gets a 10 AWG conductor and an 8 AWG EGC, and step 6 then scales from that 8 AWG. Show the computed size and don't cap it; the 250.122(A) sentence that would cap it is not verified yet. If no nec-310-16 size reaches the required area, return egc null, with egcUpsized and egcLargerThanConductor true.
